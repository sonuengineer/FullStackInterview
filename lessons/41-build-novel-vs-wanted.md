# Build Something Nobody Has Built, or Something People Already Desperately Want?

*(Personal/product judgment, kept in its own category alongside [[29-why-hire-you-over-ai]].)*

## 1. The Choice

Build something novel - nobody's built it before. Or build something people already, visibly, desperately want - even if it's not new.

## 2. The Answer: Build What's Already Desperately Wanted

Demand is the scarcest resource in building anything. Novelty without demand is a solution looking for a problem - a huge share of failed products and over-engineered internal tools share exactly this root cause: real engineering effort spent on something nobody was asking for. "Desperately want" means the risk that's hardest to de-risk later - whether anyone cares - is already answered. What's left is *execution risk*, which is far more tractable than *demand risk*.

## 3. The Nuance

"Already wanted" doesn't mean "already solved well." The winning move is almost never pure novelty *or* a straight copy - it's bringing a genuinely better approach to a need that unquestionably already exists. That still allows for real innovation; it just anchors the innovation to validated demand instead of to the excitement of being first.

## 4. The Engineering Parallel

This is the same instinct as [[30-workload-before-conclusion]] and [[15-simple-vs-scalable-architecture]]: understand the real, existing requirement before committing effort to a solution. A technically brilliant answer to a question nobody asked is exactly as wasteful in product strategy as it is in system design.

## 5. 🧠 Remember

> Novelty is a bet that demand will show up later. Building for existing, desperate demand is a bet you've already partially won - spend your actual innovation on *how* you solve it, not on *whether* anyone needed it solved.

## 6. Reflect on This Yourself

1. Think of a real product or feature you've seen fail - was it a demand problem or an execution problem?
2. Can "desperately wanted" demand still leave room for genuine innovation? Give an example.
3. Where have you personally been tempted to build the impressive thing instead of the needed thing?
